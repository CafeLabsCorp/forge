# Visual research

How `design`, `motion` and the studio look for references. Research exists to widen
what gets proposed. It fails when every piece starts from the same search.

## Where

Use **several different sources per job**, not one: Pinterest, Behance, Dribbble, Awwwards, campaign archives,
film title sequences, motion reels, and anything outside the piece's category.
Search for the idea or the feeling, not just the product type. Anything the user
already supplied comes first.

Use the browser tools when a page needs rendering; screenshots are how you see a
visual site. If a site asks for a login you can't complete, move on and say so:
don't try to get around a login or a block. Never type or store anyone's password.

## How much

A handful of good references, few consultations, no bulk downloading. Automated
collection is often against a site's terms. This is looking, not harvesting.

## What comes back

A short moodboard. For each reference: the link, a screenshot if useful, and
**the specific idea you're taking** (a concept, a technique, a movement, a way of
framing). If you can't name the idea, drop the reference.

## Rules

- References inspire; they never go into the piece. Other people's images have
  owners. Anything meant to be published needs a clear license or to be original.
- Never reproduce a reference's composition. Take one idea and combine it with
  others and with the brand's own world.
- A brand's own past pieces are checked to avoid repeating them, never as a template.
