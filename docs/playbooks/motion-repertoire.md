# Motion repertoire

What `motion` can reach for. These are **proofs of what is possible, not recipes**:
a piece that copies an entry below has failed. Pick from it by what the content
needs, and combine.

## Objects built in code

Phones, laptops, cards, documents, machines, built as real objects with depth
(CSS 3D with perspective and `preserve-3d`, or Three.js): lid opening, screen
lighting up and playing a real interface, a device turning. The screen can show a
working miniature of the product instead of a static image.

## Interfaces that act

A home screen receiving a notification, a finger or hand tapping, a form filling
itself, a cursor with intent. Tell a usage story instead of showing a screenshot.

## Characters and hands

Cut-out rigs from separate images (arm, forearm, hand pivoting), silhouettes, simple
mascots with weight and follow-through. Mind asymmetric elements: a character
with text on its body can't be mirrored.

## Camera

Entering an object and finding a world inside, one continuous flight with no cuts,
parallax in depth, push-ins timed to a word, a real perspective camera.

## Physics and feel

Springs, inertia, weight, anticipation, overshoot, squash, follow-through, things
that collide or stack. Easing is a character choice: decide it for each piece.

## Systems and data

Networks with pulses, graphs growing, counters and odometers, maps, flows that
branch and merge, charts that build an argument.

## Transitions

A thread element playing every role (a bar that becomes a progress bar, then an
underline), match cuts on shape or motion, cut on action, wipes by an object in the
scene, zoom through a letter.

## Type as image

Words that assemble from different directions, type that reacts to what's said,
type as architecture of the scene. The floor of the craft, not the point of it.

## Over footage

Graphics locked to recorded video, chroma keying, transparent overlays, tracking,
elements behind people, captions that follow the cuts.

## Choosing the tool

Simplest thing that gets the idea on screen:

- **Remotion + CSS/SVG**: most 2D work and many objects in 3D.
- **Three.js (`@remotion/three`)**: real camera, light or geometry.
- **`3d-artist`**: realistic modeling, once available.
- **Generated image or clip** (ready-to-paste prompt): photographic or illustrative
  material code wouldn't do well. Never with text inside.
- **ffmpeg**: cutting, keying and final assembly.
