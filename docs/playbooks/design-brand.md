# Design playbook: brand identity and logo

Read this when creating or revising a brand, a logo, or a brand manual.

## Start from meaning

- Before any shape: what the brand stands for, who it talks to, what it must not
  be confused with. Write the concept in one sentence; every mark must trace to it.
- **Name:** when naming is in scope, check availability before falling in love:
  domain, social handles, and the trademark registry for the country (INPI in
  Brazil). You can search; say plainly that a lawyer confirms.
- Propose 2–4 concepts that differ in idea (a metaphor, a letterform idea, an
  object), not four treatments of one idea.

## Logo craft

- Works in one color before it works in many. Design in black first.
- Survives the smallest real use (favicon, app icon, 16–32 px) and the largest.
  Test both, by rendering them.
- Built on geometry you can explain; optical corrections where geometry looks wrong.
- Deliver the levels the brand needs: full logo, symbol, wordmark, simplified.
- Vector only (SVG), text converted to outlines. Variants for light and dark
  backgrounds decided on purpose, not by inverting at the last minute.
- Check it does not resemble an existing well-known mark.

## The manual

A manual is a working tool, written so someone can make a correct piece without
asking. It contains: concept; logo with clear space, minimum size and misuse;
color in HEX, RGB and CMYK; typography with hierarchy and licensing status;
elements and imagery style; voice; applications; and a **Fixed × Series ×
Variable** section with the repertoire and the official series.

Keep a text version beside the visual one so it can be consulted cheaply.

## Deliver

Concept in a sentence, logo files by level and background, palette and type
tokens, the manual, and a real application or two (a post, a card) showing the
system holding up.
