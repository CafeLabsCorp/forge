# Design playbook: print

Read this for anything that ends on paper or a physical surface: banners, business
cards, flyers, booths, signage, stickers, packaging.

## Before designing

Get the printer's spec first: final size, bleed, color profile, file format,
resolution, and how the piece is finished (fold, cut, hem, eyelets). The spec
outranks these defaults.

## Defaults

- Typically 3 mm bleed on each side and 3–5 mm safe margin inside the cut line.
- CMYK with the printer's profile. Screen colors shift, saturated blues and
  oranges especially: warn about it, and suggest a proof for anything expensive.
- 300 dpi at final size for pieces held in the hand. Large format viewed from a
  distance can go much lower (often 72–150 dpi at final size); confirm with the
  printer.
- Vector for logos and type; fonts outlined or embedded.
- Body text in pure K black, no four-color registration on small type.

## Think in the real place

Design for the viewing distance: a banner is read from meters away in seconds; a
card is held at 30 cm. Consider what the object is near, its lighting, and what
hides parts of it (a table in front of a banner, a fold through a face).

## Deliver

Print-ready PDF to spec with bleed and crop marks when asked, the editable source,
a flat preview, and a note of everything assumed that the printer should confirm.
